\begin{abstract}
Dense well logs provide continuous indirect observations of subsurface
properties, whereas target-property curves may be available only at selected
depths. Direct supervised models can combine dense logs with sparse target
observations, but they typically use the sparse entries as input features and do
not require the reconstruction to honor them as measurements. We introduce Conditional Latent-Prior Compressed Sensing with Generative
Models (CLP-CSGM). A decoder trained on windows of the target curve supplies
the windows the reconstruction may use. The dense logs set the starting point
of the search for that window. At inference, the decoded window is adjusted
so that it matches the sparse measurements. A conditional local-recovery bound separates decoder error,
prior mismatch, measurement noise, and local separability. It does not prove
that the measurement operator satisfies S-REC on the trained decoder.
We evaluate CLP-CSGM on public Dutch North Sea well-log data reorganized into a
held-out-well clay-volume benchmark and a single-well porosity stress test with
weak dense context. The F3 evaluation uses four wells, with F02-1, F03-2, and
F06-1 for model development and F03-4 held out; the results are not presented
as operational multi-well transfer. The clay-volume target is obtained from
gamma ray by the Larionov relation, the single-well porosity target is
processed and density-derived, and the sparse observations are coordinate
subsamples of these same processed curves. The benchmarks therefore test
reconstruction of processed target logs rather than recovery against
independently acquired core or laboratory measurements. A limited-sample
permeability case provides supplementary evidence. With a linear ridge map as
the reference for that starting point, the comparison is a conditional advantage,
not overall superiority. CLP-CSGM has lower mean RMSE than the direct
autoencoder baseline in seven of nine cross-well cells, with the gains
concentrated where training support is smallest (steps $16$ and $32$). The
direct autoencoder has lower mean RMSE in the two remaining cells, both at
step $8$ (the largest training pool) for $\rho=0.05$ and $\rho=0.10$. The
unweighted mean of the nine cell-level RMSE values decreases from 0.0350 to
0.0286, an overall reduction of about 18\%. In the GR-only porosity benchmark,
CLP-CSGM has the lowest mean RMSE at the two sparsest ratios, including
0.016 versus 0.018 at $\rho=0.20$, whereas the direct autoencoder has lower
mean RMSE at $\rho=0.40$ and $\rho=0.60$. That GR-only comparison is a
controlled sparse-assimilation test under intentionally incomplete context:
gamma ray is the only dense input, and the depth profile follows the broad
trend of the processed porosity curve. It is not evidence of general porosity
reconstruction performance.
Dropping either the dense-log starting point or the match to the sparse
measurements raises the error in most cells. Which of the two matters more
changes with the number of training windows and with how many depths are
measured. The map from dense logs to the starting point is also
dataset-dependent: a multilayer perceptron gives lower mean RMSE on the
cross-well grid, whereas ridge gives lower mean RMSE when gamma ray is the
only dense log. On these processed-log tests, CLP-CSGM complements direct
regression when training windows are few, the test well differs from the
training wells, or the dense logs are incomplete.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Computer Code Availability}

CLP-CSGM (Conditional Latent-Prior Compressed Sensing with Generative Models)
was developed by Romulo Brito da Silva, LENEP, North Fluminense State
University, Rodovia Amaral Peixoto, km 163, Imboassica, Macae,
RJ 27925-310, Brazil (contact: \texttt{romulobrito@lenep.uenf.br} ;
telephone: +55 21 99101-3308).
First publicly released in 2026, it is implemented in Python 3.10 using
NumPy 1.26, pandas 2.3, Matplotlib 3.7, scikit-learn 1.6, PyTorch 2.6,
and PyWavelets 1.4. The source repository occupies approximately 10~MB,
excluding datasets and experiment outputs, and runs on a multi-core CPU
with at least 8~GB of RAM; CUDA is optional.

Source code, installation instructions, dependencies, benchmark scripts,
and documentation are available at
\url{https://github.com/romulobrito/CLP--CSGM} under the MIT License.
The repository covers the cross-well clay-volume and F03-4 porosity
benchmarks, structural ablations, latent-prior sensitivity, and
supplementary diagnostics. Processed well-log tables are not redistributed;
the README specifies the required inputs and reproduction steps.

\vspace{0.35em}
\noindent\textbf{Data availability.}
The F3-derived benchmarks follow \citet{zhang2025rockphysicslogs}; users
must obtain the tabulated inputs from the cited releases or portals under
the applicable licenses. The Lapa dataset follows
\citet{alvarez2025nonlinear}, whose underlying field data cannot be shared
publicly. Supplementary analyses therefore require locally authorized inputs.
