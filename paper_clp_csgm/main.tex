\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct a continuous target-property profile when dense auxiliary
wireline logs are available but the target is observed at only a few depths.
Direct predictors can accept those observations as features without retaining
them as explicit measurements during inference. Conditional Latent-Prior
Compressed Sensing with Generative Models (CLP-CSGM) instead combines a
learned target-window representation, a latent anchor
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$ predicted from
the dense logs, and test-time refinement against the sparse measurements.
Its local recovery bound is conditional and does not establish separation of
nearby windows by the trained decoder.

The cross-well evaluation uses three development wells and F03-4 as the
held-out well. CLP-CSGM Ridge has lower mean root
mean square error (RMSE) than a direct autoencoder in seven of nine
configurations, especially at lower training support; it also improves on its
conditional prior-only prediction in all
nine. For the GR-only porosity case, $\rho$ denotes the nominal fraction of
window depths selected for observation. CLP-CSGM is lower at
$\rho\in\{0.20,0.30\}$, the autoencoder is lower at
$\rho\in\{0.40,0.60\}$, and their errors are nearly equal at $\rho=0.50$.
At the reference noise standard deviation $0.01$, depth interpolation has
lower RMSE than CLP-CSGM in all nine cross-well configurations. The ordering
becomes more favorable to CLP-CSGM under larger synthetic perturbations that
were not calibrated to field uncertainties.

The F3 targets are processed curves: clay volume is Larionov-derived from
gamma ray, porosity is density-derived, and sparse observations are
subsamples rather than independent core or laboratory measurements. In a
separate laboratory-porosity supplement, the direct autoencoder retains lower
mean RMSE. The results support conditional benefits of explicit latent
refinement in the evaluated regimes, not general superiority, and motivate
broader validation with independently calibrated targets.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Computer Code Availability}

CLP-CSGM (Conditional Latent-Prior Compressed Sensing with Generative Models)
was developed by Romulo Brito da Silva, LENEP, North Fluminense State
University, Rodovia Amaral Peixoto, km 163, Imboassica, Macae,
RJ 27925-310, Brazil (contact: \texttt{romulobrito@lenep.uenf.br} ;
telephone: +55 21 99101-3308).
First publicly released in 2026, it is implemented in Python 3.10 using
NumPy 1.26, pandas 2.3, Matplotlib 3.7, scikit-learn 1.6, PyTorch 2.6,
and PyWavelets 1.4. The source repository occupies approximately 10~MB,
excluding datasets and experiment outputs, and runs on a multi-core CPU
with at least 8~GB of RAM; CUDA is optional.

Source code, installation instructions, dependencies, benchmark scripts,
and documentation are available at
\url{https://github.com/romulobrito/CLP--CSGM} under the MIT License.
The repository covers the cross-well clay-volume and F03-4 porosity
benchmarks, structural ablations, latent-prior sensitivity, and
supplementary diagnostics. Processed well-log tables are not redistributed;
the README specifies the required inputs and reproduction steps.

\vspace{0.35em}
\noindent\textbf{Data availability.}
The F3-derived benchmarks follow \citet{zhang2025rockphysicslogs}; users
must obtain the tabulated inputs from the cited releases or portals under
the applicable licenses. The Lapa dataset follows
\citet{alvarez2025nonlinear}, whose underlying field data cannot be shared
publicly. Supplementary analyses therefore require locally authorized inputs.
