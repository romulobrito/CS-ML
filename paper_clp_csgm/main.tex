\begin{abstract}
Reconstructing a petrophysical target curve is difficult when dense auxiliary
logs are available along the well but the target is observed only at selected
depths. Direct models can use those sparse observations as input features,
yet they do not require the reconstruction to honor them as measurements.
We introduce Conditional Latent-Prior Compressed Sensing with Generative
Models (CLP-CSGM). A decoder trained on windows of the target curve supplies
the windows the reconstruction may use. Dense auxiliary logs predict a
conditional latent code, $z_0$. Latent refinement balances disagreement with
the sparse measurements against proximity to $z_0$. That code is the
reference for this balance, not only a starting value. A residual at the
observed depths can remain.
The cross-well evaluation uses public Dutch North Sea logs, with three wells
for model development and one well held out. The main targets are derived:
clay volume from gamma ray by the Larionov relation, and a processed,
density-derived porosity. Sparse observations are coordinate subsamples of
these same curves rather than independently acquired core or laboratory
measurements.
CLP-CSGM has lower mean root-mean-square error than the direct autoencoder
in seven of nine cross-well configurations, with the gains concentrated in
the smallest training set. This comparison is a conditional advantage over
that autoencoder, not superiority over every comparator. At the reference
noise standard deviation 0.01, depth interpolation of the sparse measurements
has lower error than CLP-CSGM in all nine configurations. Under larger
perturbations that interpolation comparison changes. These noise levels were
used for sensitivity analysis and were not calibrated to field measurement
uncertainties. In a supplementary laboratory-porosity check, the direct
autoencoder has the lower mean error. The benefit depends on the regime, and
broader validation is still required.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Computer Code Availability}

CLP-CSGM (Conditional Latent-Prior Compressed Sensing with Generative Models)
was developed by Romulo Brito da Silva, LENEP, North Fluminense State
University, Rodovia Amaral Peixoto, km 163, Imboassica, Macae,
RJ 27925-310, Brazil (contact: \texttt{romulobrito@lenep.uenf.br} ;
telephone: +55 21 99101-3308).
First publicly released in 2026, it is implemented in Python 3.10 using
NumPy 1.26, pandas 2.3, Matplotlib 3.7, scikit-learn 1.6, PyTorch 2.6,
and PyWavelets 1.4. The source repository occupies approximately 10~MB,
excluding datasets and experiment outputs, and runs on a multi-core CPU
with at least 8~GB of RAM; CUDA is optional.

Source code, installation instructions, dependencies, benchmark scripts,
and documentation are available at
\url{https://github.com/romulobrito/CLP--CSGM} under the MIT License.
The repository covers the cross-well clay-volume and F03-4 porosity
benchmarks, structural ablations, latent-prior sensitivity, and
supplementary diagnostics. Processed well-log tables are not redistributed;
the README specifies the required inputs and reproduction steps.

\vspace{0.35em}
\noindent\textbf{Data availability.}
The F3-derived benchmarks follow \citet{zhang2025rockphysicslogs}; users
must obtain the tabulated inputs from the cited releases or portals under
the applicable licenses. The Lapa dataset follows
\citet{alvarez2025nonlinear}, whose underlying field data cannot be shared
publicly. Supplementary analyses therefore require locally authorized inputs.
