\section{Interpretation, scope, and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The methodological distinction of CLP-CSGM is not the latent representation
alone, but the way dense context and sparse target measurements enter
inference. Dense logs determine the anchor
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$, while the sparse values remain in an
explicit measurement residual during latent refinement. This separates the
method from direct $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ prediction and from
approaches that complete a missing log interval, translate one property image
to another, or regress a target from the available logs
\citep{koeshidayatullah2026timegpt,alfakih2026pix2geomodel,
alfakih2025wellloggan,alfakih2023northsea}. The decoder range determines what
can be represented; the anchor guides the search within that range; and the
observations can correct the conditioned prediction without being fitted
exactly.

The ablations provide the clearest internal evidence for this design. In the
cross-well grid, the full Ridge model has lower mean RMSE than prior-only in
all nine cells and lower mean RMSE than measurement-only CSGM in seven. At
steps $16$ and $32$ it is lower than both ablations at every tested ratio,
whereas measurement-only inversion is lower in the two step-$8$ exceptions.
On the F03-4 GR-only grid, the full model is lower than both ablations at all
five ratios. Sparse-measurement refinement therefore improves the conditioned
prediction consistently in these experiments, while the value of the
conditional anchor depends on the configuration.

The external comparisons identify where that internal gain translates into
better prediction. CLP-CSGM Ridge is lower than the direct AE in seven of
nine cross-well cells, including all cells at steps $16$ and $32$. The
additional seed and measurement-realization checks preserve that ordering
within the same held-out well. Because the sliding step also changes window
sampling and overlap, the result supports the complete procedure in those
limited-support configurations rather than a causal effect of training-set
size alone. The depth profiles complement this aggregate evidence: the
multi-channel model follows the broad $V_c$ variation in the displayed
cross-well interval, while the GR-only porosity reconstruction is smoother
and misses some local structure. Attainable detail depends jointly on the
dense-log information, the decoder range, and the number and placement of
sparse observations.

The class of the conditional map also matters. Under matched channels and
splits, the MLP prior is lower than Ridge in seven of nine cross-well cells
and reduces the unweighted mean RMSE from $0.02862$ to $0.02538$ ($11.3\%$).
Its advantage narrows as training support decreases, and Ridge is lower at
step $32$ for $\rho=0.10$ and $\rho=0.20$. The ordering reverses on the
controlled F03-4 grid, where Ridge is lower at all five ratios. Ridge remains
the primary reference because it supplies one fixed linear low-capacity map
across the main grid, not because it is uniformly preferable. The class of
$h$, latent dimension, and regularization should consequently be chosen on
validation data for the intended measurements; the experiments do not isolate
model capacity as the cause of the observed ranking.

Simple reconstruction provides an important counterpoint. At the reference
noise standard deviation $0.01$, depth interpolation is lower than CLP-CSGM
in all nine cross-well cells, and the validation-selected second-difference
smoother is lower in eight. In this regime the sparse observations already
support lower-RMSE depth-domain reconstruction in this benchmark, so
iterative latent refinement adds cost without improving RMSE. As the
synthetic perturbation increases,
CLP-CSGM becomes lower than interpolation in seven cells at $0.05$ and all
nine at $0.10$ and $0.20$; against smoothing, the corresponding counts are
five, eight, and nine. This pattern is relative robustness of the complete
procedure, with $\lambda$ reselected by validation at each level. Decoder
restriction, conditional penalization, and parameter reselection are all
compatible with the behavior, but the sweep does not isolate their individual
effects.

The larger perturbations are sensitivity levels rather than estimates of
field uncertainty. An instrument-aware analysis would require gamma-ray and
endpoint uncertainties to be propagated through the nonlinear Larionov
relation. Moreover, AE, ordinary least squares, and direct ridge were not
rerun across the sweep. The result therefore concerns the reported
comparisons with interpolation and smoothing, not a universal noise threshold
or general superiority under noisy measurements. Computational cost reinforces
this conditional interpretation: CLP-CSGM uses iterative inference, whereas
direct predictors require one forward pass. Its additional cost is most
relevant when explicit sparse-measurement refinement yields a meaningful
accuracy gain; faster solvers, adaptive stopping, and amortized refinement
remain useful directions.

The benchmark provenance defines a second boundary on interpretation.
Cross-well $V_c$ is a processed Larionov-derived curve, so gamma ray is
withheld from the main input to avoid supplying the source of the target.
When gamma ray is available, the conventional relation can be evaluated
directly. Restoring that channel lowers absolute error without changing the
step-$16$ ordering among CLP-CSGM Ridge, direct AE, and measurement-only CSGM.
The F03-4 porosity target is likewise processed and density-derived; sonic is
excluded because it follows that curve almost one to one, and gamma ray is
the only dense input. In both F3 settings, sparse observations are subsamples
of the processed target rather than independently acquired property
measurements. The experiments therefore test reconstruction under deliberately
restricted information, not replacement of the Larionov or porosity relations
in a fully logged well.

A field analogue would combine a missing or unreliable source log with a few
independently acquired target measurements, but the present benchmarks
reproduce only its restricted-input and sparse-observation structure. The
Lapa permeability case \citep{alvarez2025nonlinear} extends the evaluation to
another lithology and target property with a small sample. The laboratory
porosity supplement uses a target not deterministically derived from the
auxiliary logs by this pipeline: CLP-CSGM improves on the decoded conditional
prior in every block--seed pair, while direct AE has the lower aggregate mean
RMSE at both ratios. One cored interval, within-window withheld coordinates,
and overlapping training windows limit the scope of that evidence. Broader
transfer assessment, such as the cross-basin setting studied by
\citet{koeshidayatullah2026timegpt}, will require additional wells, log suites,
independently calibrated targets, and geological labels.

The available metrics are not stratified by lithofacies, texture, or formal
stratigraphic interval because independent labels are unavailable at each
depth sample. RMSE, MAE, and relative $\ell_2$ error consequently aggregate
heterogeneous windows, while the depth profiles provide qualitative rather
than facies-resolved audits. Such resolution would require independently
interpreted or core-calibrated interval labels, particularly because decoder
adequacy and prior accuracy may vary across geological regimes.

Finally, the theoretical statements retain their conditional scope.
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional_local_recovery} separates decoder mismatch,
latent-prior mismatch, measurement noise, optimization quality, and local
separability, but assumes rather than proves a local S-REC condition for the
trained decoder. The sampled diagnostics in
Appendix~\ref{app:separability} therefore support interpretation of the
decoder outputs without providing an operator-level recovery guarantee.
