\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Petrophysical log prediction}

Well logs provide dense but indirect measurements of rock and fluid properties,
and data-driven methods are widely used to predict petrophysical targets
\citep{ellis2007well,asquith2004basic,ahmadi2019comparison,wood2020predicting}.
Recent studies address three neighboring tasks.

Log completion reconstructs missing intervals of a wireline curve from that
curve. Sequence-based GANs generate synthetic logs and fill gaps in gamma ray,
sonic, neutron porosity, deep resistivity, and bulk density
\citep{alfakih2025wellloggan}. A fine-tuned time-series foundation model
forecasts the next interval of the same suite and flags anomalies, including a
zero-shot check in a second basin \citep{koeshidayatullah2026timegpt}. The
unknown is a missing piece of the log, and the trained predictor fills it in
one pass.

Geomodel translation reconstructs a reservoir-property image from another
image. A Pix2Pix conditional GAN maps two-dimensional slices of facies,
porosity, permeability, and water saturation, taking either a masked image of
the same property or a different property as generator input
\citep{alfakih2026pix2geomodel}. Agreement with the supplied image is learned
in training.

Direct property regression maps wireline logs to a petrophysical target. ANN
and random-forest models predict total porosity, effective porosity, and shale
volume from logs that include neutron porosity \citep{alfakih2023northsea}.
Missing log values are treated in preprocessing. At inference the model returns
the property from the logs, and target values act as training labels.

CLP-CSGM recovers a window of a target property when dense logs are available
along the window and only sparse observations of that property are in hand.
The conditional map predicts $\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$ from the
dense logs. This code initializes the first latent trajectory and remains the
center of the quadratic penalty. The sparse values are measurements
$\boldsymbol{b}$. After training, latent refinement balances agreement with
$\boldsymbol{b}$ against deviation from $\boldsymbol{z}_0$. A residual at the
observed depths can remain. Log completion and geomodel
translation fill a curve or a map from context. Direct regression predicts the
property from the logs. CLP-CSGM keeps an explicit measurement penalty
at inference. These tasks can all exploit available context, including
observed portions of a well log or a partially masked property image. The
principal distinction is therefore not the mere availability of known values,
but how these values enter the reconstruction procedure. The cited completion
and translation methods primarily learn mappings for gap filling or property
prediction. In CLP-CSGM, dense auxiliary logs determine a conditional latent
anchor, while the sparse target observations enter an explicit
measurement-fidelity penalty during test-time latent refinement. This
distinction concerns the inference formulation and does not establish
superior predictive performance over the cited approaches, whose targets,
observation patterns, and evaluation protocols differ.

The experiments use the processed F3 block data of
\citet{zhang2025rockphysicslogs}, who combine supervised prediction with
Gardner and Larionov relations and SHAP-based interpretation. We use these logs
to study reconstruction from dense context while penalizing disagreement
with sparse target-well observations.


\subsection{Classical and generative compressed sensing}

Classical compressed sensing combines an explicit measurement operator with a
sparsity or compressibility prior
\citep{donoho2006compressed,candes2006robust,candes2006stable}. Here,
$M_\Omega$ is a coordinate restriction selecting observed target depths. A fixed
transform-domain prior may be restrictive for cross-well petrophysical windows,
whose representation can vary across wells. Restriction operators are also common
in seismic, ultrasonic, and sparse-sensor reconstruction
\citep{herrmann2008nonparametric,hennenfent2008simplydenoise,deAssis2022CompressiveSF,
muir2021seismicwavefield,dasilva2025csshredenhancingshredrobust}.

CSGM replaces fixed-basis sparsity with the range of a
learned generator or decoder \citep{bora2017compressed}. Reconstruction searches
for a latent code $\boldsymbol{z}$ whose decoded signal reduces the
measurement residual.
Recovery properties and limitations of this learned-range prior have been studied
under set-restricted conditions and related generative formulations
\citep{bora2017compressed,kamath2020power,asim2020invertible}. CLP-CSGM retains
this decoder-range view and adds dense-log conditioning of the latent search.



\subsection{Conditional generative inverse problems and positioning}

Conditional approaches differ in where side information and measurements enter.
Direct conditional predictors map side information, alone or with observations,
to an output or latent code \citep{mirza2014conditional,sohn2015learning}, while
measurement-conditional CSGM conditions the generator directly on compressed
measurements and their sampling pattern \citep{kim2021measurementconditional}.
Other formulations retain an explicit inverse problem: side information may
regularize a measurement-consistent reconstruction
\citep{ongie2020deep,lucas2018using}, approximate or sample inverse posteriors
\citep{adler2018deepbayes,altekruger2024robustcgm,bendel2024rcgan}, or use
score/diffusion models for inverse reconstruction
\citep{dasgupta2024mechanicsdiffusion,jacobsen2025cocogen}.

CLP-CSGM belongs to this explicit conditional inverse-reconstruction setting.
Dense logs predict $\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$ before the search.
This code initializes the first trajectory and remains the center of the
quadratic penalty. The other restarts begin at small perturbations of
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$. The sparse target values enter only through the
measurement residual. The experiments therefore compare a direct map
from $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ to the window, the decoded prediction
of the conditional prior $G(\boldsymbol{z}_0)$, and a reconstruction that uses
only the measurements.
Measurement-conditional generators and posterior-sampling architectures remain
outside the empirical scope.
