\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

CLP-CSGM uses three pieces. A decoder, trained on windows of the target
curve, supplies the windows the reconstruction may use. The conditional map
predicts a latent code $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ from the dense auxiliary logs. This
code provides an informed initialization and serves as the reference for the
quadratic penalty during latent refinement. Latent refinement balances
agreement with the sparse observations against deviation from
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$. A residual at the observed depths can remain. The decoder
range and the quadratic penalty are different components.
This section defines the measurement model, the objective, the comparisons
that drop one piece, and the error bound.

\subsection{Problem setup}
\label{subsec:problem_setup}

Let $\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ denote a target-property window of $N$
depth samples and let $\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$ denote the vectorized
dense-log channels over the same interval. Target values are observed at the
ordered coordinate set
$\Omega=\{\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_m\}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}$, where
$1\leq m<N$ and the measurement ratio is $\rho=m/N$.

The coordinate-restriction operator
$M_{\Omega}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}$ is represented by

\begin{equation}
M_{\Omega}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\boldsymbol{e}_{\omega_1}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{e}_{\omega_m}^{\top}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times N},
\qquad
M_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{y}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
y_{\omega_1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{\omega_m}
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:measurement_operator}
\end{equation}

where $\boldsymbol{e}_i$ is the $i$-th canonical basis vector of
$\mathbb{R}^{N}$. Coordinate restriction is widely used to represent incomplete
seismic, ultrasonic, and sparse-sensor observations
\citep{herrmann2008nonparametric,deAssis2022CompressiveSF,
dasilva2025csshredenhancingshredrobust}.

The sparse target measurements satisfy

\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{b}
=
M_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{y}
+
\boldsymbol{\eta},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},
\label{eq:measurement_model}
\end{equation}

where $\boldsymbol{\eta}$ is additive noise. Under the Gaussian model,
$\boldsymbol{\eta}\sim
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\sigma_{\eta}^{2}I_m)$, where $I_m$ is the
$m\times m$ identity; the noiseless case has
$\boldsymbol{\eta}=\boldsymbol{0}$.

The dense logs and the sparse measurements do different jobs. The dense-log
vector $\boldsymbol{u}$ predicts the code
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$, where $h$ is the conditional map from
dense logs to that code. The measurements $\boldsymbol{b}$ enter
only as the penalized mismatch in Eq.~\eqref{eq:clp_csgm_objective}.
The first latent trajectory starts at $\boldsymbol{z}_0$, and the penalty
center remains $\boldsymbol{z}_0$.



\paragraph{Standardization and modeling assumptions.}
All preprocessing statistics are computed from training windows only. Let
$\boldsymbol{\mu}_y,\boldsymbol{s}_y$ and
$\boldsymbol{\mu}_u,\boldsymbol{s}_u$ be the componentwise training means and
standard deviations, with $D_y=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{s}_y)$ and
$D_u=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{s}_u)$. These two matrices rescale each
coordinate by its training standard deviation. They are not estimated
covariance matrices of the profiles, and this rescaling leaves any
correlation already present between coordinates unchanged. We use
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol{y}}=D_y^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_y)$ and
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}=D_u^{-1}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_u)$.
Sparse measurements are standardized with
$D_{\Omega}=M_{\Omega}D_yM_{\Omega}^{\top}$ and
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}=D_{\Omega}^{-1}
(\boldsymbol{b}-M_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mu}_y)$; near-zero standard deviations
use the implementation safeguard. The decoder $G$ returns a standardized
window. The same window in the original target units is

\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z})
=
\boldsymbol{\mu}_y
+
D_yG(\boldsymbol{z}),
\label{eq:g_orig}
\end{equation}

and a standardized prediction returns to that scale by

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{y}}
=
\boldsymbol{\mu}_y
+
D_y\widehat{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{y}}}.
\label{eq:inverse_target_standardization}
\end{equation} For a fixed set
of observed coordinates, Eq.~\eqref{eq:measurement_model} adds noise with
$\boldsymbol{\eta}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\sigma_{\eta}^{2}I_m)$.
Independence across coordinates and a common variance are assumptions of
this synthetic benchmark noise. They are not a demonstrated description of
instrumental noise, and this manuscript does not identify them with the
30dB filename label. The standardized noise is
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=D_{\Omega}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}$. Because
$D_{\Omega}$ is diagonal,

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})
=
\sigma_{\eta}^{2}D_{\Omega}^{-1}D_{\Omega}^{-\top}
=
\sigma_{\eta}^{2}D_{\Omega}^{-2}.
\label{eq:standardized_noise_covariance}
\end{equation}

This diagonal transformation preserves the independence of the added
Gaussian noise. When the training standard deviations differ across
coordinates, the standardized variances differ as well. For
$\boldsymbol{r}=M_{\Omega}G(\boldsymbol{z})-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}$,

\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{r}^{\top}
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})^{-1}
\boldsymbol{r}
=
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}
\|D_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{r}\|_2^2.
\label{eq:standardized_noise_likelihood}
\end{equation}

The right-hand side is the data term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:clp_csgm_objective} divided by $\sigma_{\eta}^{2}$.
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_residual_identity}, that data term equals
$\|M_{\Omega}G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z})-\boldsymbol{b}\|_2^2$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:map} reads
$\|M_{\Omega}G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z})-\boldsymbol{b}\|_2^2
+\lambda\|\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_0\|_2^2$
as a MAP objective in which the data term has coefficient one and
$\lambda=\sigma_{\eta}^{2}/\sigma_{z}^{2}$ multiplies the latent penalty.
The factor
$\sigma_z^{2}I_k$ in that proposition is the isotropic hypothesis behind
the Euclidean latent penalty. It is not an estimated covariance of the
residuals of $h$. Ridge standardizes the latent codes while fitting $h$
and returns $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ to the encoder scale.

Where a later equation omits tildes, its coordinates are those written there.
The refinement objective keeps the standardized residual and the diagonal
weight $D_{\Omega}$. The decoder
$G:\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}$, with $k<N$, supplies
standardized windows, and $G_{\mathrm{orig}}$ supplies the corresponding
windows in the original target units. We assume that target windows are
adequately approximated by the windows $G_{\mathrm{orig}}$ can return, and that
$h:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}$ provides a useful anchor
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$. These assumptions are assessed empirically
through reconstruction, ablations, and prior sensitivity.



\subsection{Learned model components}
\label{subsec:learned_priors}

Offline training fits an autoencoder on standardized target windows and a
latent-prior predictor on paired dense-log windows and encoded targets. All
learned components remain frozen during target-well reconstruction.

\paragraph{Generative decoder.}
Let
$E_{\theta_E}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}$ and
$G_{\theta_G}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}$, with $k<N$, denote
the encoder and decoder. Given standardized training targets
$\{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_i\}_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}$, their parameters
are obtained from

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\widehat{\theta}_E,\widehat{\theta}_G)
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\theta_E,\theta_G}
\;&
\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}
\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}
\left\|
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_i
-
G_{\theta_G}
\left(
E_{\theta_E}
\left(
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_i
\right)
\right)
\right\|_2^2
\\
&+
\gamma_{\mathrm{AE}}
\left(
\|\theta_E\|_2^2+\|\theta_G\|_2^2
\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:autoencoder_training_objective}
\end{equation}

where $n_{\mathrm{tr}}$ is the number of training windows and
$\gamma_{\mathrm{AE}}\geq 0$ is the autoencoder regularization weight. After
training, we write $E=E_{\widehat{\theta}_E}$ and
$G=G_{\widehat{\theta}_G}$. The frozen decoder defines the learned
target-window classes

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{G}
=
\left\{
G(\boldsymbol{z}):
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
\right\}
\subseteq
\mathbb{R}^{N},
\qquad
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig}}
=
\left\{
G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z}):
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
\right\}
\subseteq
\mathbb{R}^{N}.
\label{eq:decoder_range}
\end{equation}

$\mathcal{R}_{G}$ is the range of the standardized decoder.
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig}}$ is that class in the original target units.
$M_{\Omega}$ selects observed coordinates and is not either set.
Target-well reconstruction therefore optimizes only
$\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}$ and produces
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{y}}
=G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}})
\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig}}$; it does not update the network
parameters or search over arbitrary vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$.
The encoder is used to train the decoder and to build the codes on which
the dense-log map is fit. Inference does not encode the unknown window.
In the reported model, $k=16$ is the dimension of that code.
A reconstruction $G(E(\boldsymbol{y}))$ that is given the full target window
checks whether the decoder can represent that window. It uses target values
that inference does not have, so it is not one of the compared predictors.
Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_simple_baselines} reports that check.

\paragraph{Log-conditioned latent prior.}
Each training target is encoded as
$\boldsymbol{z}_i=E(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_i)$, and
$h:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}$ is fitted from
$\{(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_i,\boldsymbol{z}_i)\}_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}$.
For the Ridge variant,
$h(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}})
=W\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}+\boldsymbol{c}$, with parameters

\begin{equation}
(\widehat{W},\widehat{\boldsymbol{c}})
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{W,\boldsymbol{c}}
\left\{
\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}
\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}
\left\|
\boldsymbol{z}_i
-
W\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_i
-
\boldsymbol{c}
\right\|_2^2
+
\alpha\|W\|_F^2
\right\},
\label{eq:ridge_latent_training_objective}
\end{equation}

where $\alpha\geq 0$ controls the Ridge penalty
\citep{hoerl1970ridge}. After fitting,
$h(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}})
=\widehat{W}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
+\widehat{\boldsymbol{c}}$, and, suppressing tildes, the contextual anchor is
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}$. The decoder $G$
determines which target windows can be represented, and $h$ uses dense-log
context to center the latent search. CLP-CSGM is this scheme; the class of
$h$ is a configuration choice. Ridge is the primary reference because it is a
linear map from the dense logs to $\boldsymbol{z}_0$, not because it is the
best map in every test. An MLP variant of $h$ is compared under the same
channels and the same split in Section~\ref{subsec:latent_prior_sensitivity}.

\subsection{Test-time reconstruction}
\label{subsec:test_time_reconstruction}

At inference time, $G$ and $h$ remain fixed, and only the latent code of the
current target window is optimized. Given standardized dense logs
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$ and sparse target observations
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, define
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}})$. CLP-CSGM computes

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{num}}
&\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
\left\{
\left\|
D_{\Omega}
\left(
M_{\Omega}G(\boldsymbol{z})
-
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}
\right)
\right\|_2^2
+
\lambda
\left\|
\boldsymbol{z}
-
\boldsymbol{z}_0
\right\|_2^2
\right\},
\\
\widehat{\boldsymbol{y}}
&=
G_{\mathrm{orig}}\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{num}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:clp_csgm_objective}
\end{equation}

The first term penalizes disagreement with the sparse observations and the
second, weighted by $\lambda\geq 0$, penalizes deviation from
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$. Their sum does not force
the observations to be met exactly, so a residual at the observed depths can
remain. Because each row of $M_{\Omega}$ selects one window coordinate,

\begin{equation}
\left\|
D_{\Omega}
\left(
M_{\Omega}G(\boldsymbol{z})
-
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}
\right)
\right\|_2^2
=
\left\|
M_{\Omega}G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z})
-
\boldsymbol{b}
\right\|_2^2.
\label{eq:weighted_residual_identity}
\end{equation}

The left-hand side is the data term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:clp_csgm_objective}. The right-hand side is the squared
residual in the original target units, which is the residual evaluated by
the implementation. The diagonal entries of $D_y$ are the training standard
deviations of the window coordinates. On the cross-well $V_c$ training
windows those entries are not equal: the ratio of the largest to the
smallest is about $1.13$ at step $8$, $1.23$ at step $16$, and $1.40$ at
step $32$. A single factor therefore does not convert a penalty weight
between the unweighted standardized residual and this objective. The
implementation also divides both terms by the number of windows. Multiplying
the entire objective by the same positive factor leaves its minimizers
unchanged. $\lambda\geq0$ is selected on validation
windows for each measurement ratio and seed.
Proposition~\ref{prop:map} identifies $\lambda$ with
$\sigma_{\eta}^{2}/\sigma_{z}^{2}$ when the likelihood is an isotropic
Gaussian in those original target units. That is the scale of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_residual_identity}. The same ratio does not apply to
the unweighted standardized residual
$\|M_{\Omega}G(\boldsymbol{z})-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_2^2$. The
reported experiments do not compute $\lambda$ from those variances. For
each seed and each measurement ratio, validation full-window RMSE selects
one candidate from
$\{10^{-4},3\cdot10^{-4},10^{-3},3\cdot10^{-3},10^{-2},3\cdot10^{-2},10^{-1}\}$,
and that choice is fixed before the test score is computed. The selected
$\lambda$ is the validation choice for the original-scale objective.
Because $G$ is nonlinear, the
objective is generally nonconvex and is solved with Adam
\citep{kingma2015adam}. The first run starts from $\boldsymbol{z}_0$. Each
additional restart starts at $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ plus an independent draw from
$\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},0.05^{2}I)$, with the same shape as
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$. The penalty center remains $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ on every
restart and every iteration, and the lowest-objective candidate is
retained. Thus $\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{num}}$ is a numerical
solution, not a certified global minimizer.

\paragraph{Ablation variants.}
The \emph{latent-prior-only} variant suppresses refinement and uses
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{prior}}=\boldsymbol{z}_0$, followed by
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{y}}_{\mathrm{prior}}
=G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{prior}})$.
The \emph{measurement-only CSGM} variant sets $\lambda=0$ and computes

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{meas}}
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
\left\|
D_{\Omega}
\left(
M_{\Omega}G(\boldsymbol{z})
-
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}
\right)
\right\|_2^2.
\label{eq:measurement_only_ablation}
\end{equation}

The latent-prior-only variant returns the decoded prediction of the
conditional prior, $G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z}_0)$, with no latent
search. In that
variant $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ is not an initialization, because no trajectory is
optimized. The
measurement-only variant uses only the sparse measurements and recovers
measurement-only generative compressed sensing \citep{bora2017compressed}.
Comparison with the full objective in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:clp_csgm_objective} shows what each piece contributes. Results
are reported in Section~\ref{subsec:ablation_analysis}.

\subsection{Interpretation and theoretical context}
\label{subsec:interpretation_scope}

The results below answer three questions about the method. How should the
two terms of the objective be interpreted? Under what conditions does the
problem have a minimizer? Which factors condition recovery of the full
window from the sparse observations? The MAP/Tikhonov reading and the
existence argument are classical. They are used to interpret the
formulation and are not presented as new theoretical contributions.
Complete proofs appear in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}.

Under the Gaussian hypotheses in Proposition~\ref{prop:map}, the objective
admits a MAP interpretation. The quadratic penalty is centered at the
conditioned code $\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$, not at the origin
\citep{tikhonov1963solution,burger2014map}. In that reading the data term
has coefficient one, and
$\lambda=\sigma_{\eta}^{2}/\sigma_{z}^{2}$ multiplies the latent penalty.
The data term is the original-scale residual, which
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_residual_identity} identifies with the standardized
residual weighted by $D_{\Omega}$. The reported experiments do not estimate
this variance ratio. Validation selects $\lambda$, as stated in
Section~\ref{subsec:test_time_reconstruction}. The MAP reading explains the
form of the penalty. It does not demonstrate empirical superiority.

\begin{remark}[Relation to classical CSGM]
\label{rem:csgm_relation}
Setting $\lambda=0$ recovers the measurement-only CSGM problem in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:measurement_only_ablation}
\citep{bora2017compressed}. Increasing $\lambda$ shifts the reconstruction
toward the decoded prediction of the conditional prior,
$G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z}_0)$, by penalizing departures
from $\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$ more strongly. That shift is not
a claim that a larger $\lambda$ improves the reconstruction.
\end{remark}

Proposition~\ref{prop:existence} is a classical existence result. If the
decoder is continuous and $\lambda>0$, the objective is continuous and
coercive, so it attains a minimum. The result does not give uniqueness,
convexity, or convergence of Adam to a global minimizer. It is stated for
$\lambda>0$ and does not extend automatically to the measurement-only case
$\lambda=0$. For any global minimizer $\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}$,
$\mathcal{J}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}})
\leq
\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{z}_0)
=
\left\|
D_{\Omega}
\left(
M_{\Omega}G(\boldsymbol{z}_0)
-
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}
\right)
\right\|_2^2$.
Exact minimization therefore cannot worsen the optimized objective relative
to the latent-prior-only candidate. It does not guarantee lower error with
respect to the unknown target. For the numerical solution
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{num}}$, this comparison holds only when
the computed values satisfy
$\mathcal{J}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{num}})
\leq
\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{z}_0)$.
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}_{\mathrm{num}}$ remains a numerical solution, not
a certified global minimizer.


\begin{remark}[Representation error and decoder mismatch]
\label{rem:representation_error}
Every CLP-CSGM reconstruction in the original target units satisfies
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{y}}
=G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}})
\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig}}$.
Consequently, for an unknown target window
$\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$, the error of any such
reconstruction is bounded below by the oracle representation error

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{dist}
\left(
\boldsymbol{y}^{\star},
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig}}
\right)
:=
\inf_{\boldsymbol{y}'\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig}}}
\left\|
\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}
-
\boldsymbol{y}'
\right\|_2
=
\inf_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
\left\|
\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}
-
G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z})
\right\|_2.
\label{eq:oracle_decoder_error}
\end{equation}

Thus, even with noiseless observations and exact global optimization, latent
refinement cannot remove mismatch between
$\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}$ and $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig}}$. This
structural error is represented by $\boldsymbol{r}^{\dagger}$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional_local_recovery} and is distinct from the
latent-prior mismatch
$\|\boldsymbol{z}^{\dagger}-\boldsymbol{z}_0\|_2$.

An inaccurate code
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$ may bias the search toward a suboptimal
latent region, because it initializes the first trajectory and remains the
center of the penalty. The data-fidelity term can partially correct this bias when the
sparse observations are informative and $\lambda$ does not dominate the
objective. The decoder range determines what can be represented; the
conditional code $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ determines where the latent penalty is
centered.
\end{remark}

Four factors enter the bound in
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional_local_recovery}. Representation error is
the difference between the true window and a window the decoder can
represent. Noise and observed discrepancy combine the measurement
perturbations with the part of that representation error present at the
observed coordinates. Deviation from the conditioned code is the distance
between a reference code $\boldsymbol{z}^{\dagger}$ and the penalty center
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$. Local separability is the ability of the observed
coordinates to distinguish different windows produced by the decoder in
the region under consideration. The statement below assigns symbols to
these factors.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional local recovery interpretation]
\label{prop:conditional_local_recovery}
Let
$\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ satisfy
$\boldsymbol{b}
=M_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}+\boldsymbol{\eta}$, let
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$, $\lambda>0$, and let
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}}$ be any global minimizer of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:clp_csgm_objective}. The residual
$\|M_{\Omega}G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z})-\boldsymbol{b}\|_2^2$
is the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_residual_identity}.
For any reference code
$\boldsymbol{z}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}$, define

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{r}^{\dagger}
&=
\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}
-
G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\boldsymbol{z}^{\dagger}),
&
\boldsymbol{e}^{\dagger}
&=
M_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{r}^{\dagger}
+
\boldsymbol{\eta},
\\
\Delta^{\dagger}
&=
\left(
\|\boldsymbol{e}^{\dagger}\|_2^2
+
\lambda
\|\boldsymbol{z}^{\dagger}-\boldsymbol{z}_0\|_2^2
\right)^{1/2},
&
R^{\dagger}
&=
\frac{\Delta^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:local_recovery_quantities}
\end{equation}

Let
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig},R^{\dagger}}
=
G_{\mathrm{orig}}(B(\boldsymbol{z}_0,R^{\dagger}))$, where
$B(\boldsymbol{z}_0,R^{\dagger})
=
\left\{
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:
\|\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_0\|_2
\leq R^{\dagger}
\right\}$.
The constants $\gamma_R>0$ and $\delta_R\geq0$ below refer to pairs of
windows in $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig},R^{\dagger}}$. They are not constants of the
standardized decoder $G$. If

\begin{equation}
\left\|
M_{\Omega}
(\boldsymbol{y}_1-\boldsymbol{y}_2)
\right\|_2
\geq
\gamma_R
\left\|
\boldsymbol{y}_1-\boldsymbol{y}_2
\right\|_2
-
\delta_R,
\qquad
\forall\,
\boldsymbol{y}_1,\boldsymbol{y}_2
\in
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orig},R^{\dagger}},
\label{eq:local_srec_condition}
\end{equation}

then

\begin{equation}
\left\|
G_{\mathrm{orig}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{z}})
-
\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}
\right\|_2
\leq
\|\boldsymbol{r}^{\dagger}\|_2
+
\frac{
\Delta^{\dagger}
+
\|\boldsymbol{e}^{\dagger}\|_2
+
\delta_R
}{
\gamma_R
}.
\label{eq:local_recovery_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional_local_recovery} organizes factors that
can contribute to the reconstruction error. It is a sufficient conditional
interpretation, not a verified guarantee for the trained decoder. The
latent penalty localizes the S-REC condition around $\boldsymbol{z}_0$,
and the bound follows the decoder-range view of generative compressed
sensing \citep{candes2006stable,bora2017compressed}. The bound depends on
the local separability condition stated above. Coordinate restriction is
not proved to satisfy S-REC on the trained decoder range; this depends on
local decoder secants. The empirical separability diagnostics in
Appendix~\ref{app:separability} do not certify that condition for every
