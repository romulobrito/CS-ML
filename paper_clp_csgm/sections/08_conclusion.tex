\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CLP-CSGM combines a learned target-window representation with two sources of
information that retain distinct roles at inference: dense auxiliary logs
predict a latent anchor, and sparse target observations refine that prediction
through an explicit measurement residual. The resulting MAP/Tikhonov
interpretation clarifies the balance between the two terms, while the local
error bound separates decoder mismatch, prior mismatch, measurement noise,
and local separability under stated conditions.

In the cross-well F3 benchmark, CLP-CSGM Ridge achieves lower mean RMSE than
the direct autoencoder baseline AE
$[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ in seven of nine configurations, including
every tested ratio at steps $16$ and $32$. Across the grid, the unweighted
mean decreases from $0.03496$ to $0.02862$, an $18.1\%$ reduction. Because
the sliding step also changes window sampling and overlap, this result
characterizes the complete procedure in the evaluated limited-support
configurations rather than a causal effect of training-set size alone. The
ablations show more directly what refinement contributes: the full model is
lower than prior-only in all
nine cross-well cells and lower than measurement-only CSGM in seven. On the
F03-4 GR-only grid, it is lower than both ablations at every ratio, while its
ordering against the direct AE changes with the observed fraction. The
opposite Ridge--MLP rankings across the two benchmarks further show that the
appropriate conditional map depends on the data regime.

Simple methods define the other side of that result. At the reference noise
standard deviation $0.01$, depth interpolation is lower than CLP-CSGM in all
nine cross-well cells and the validation-selected smoother is lower in eight.
When sparse observations already support lower-RMSE depth-domain
reconstruction, the additional latent optimization is not justified by RMSE.
Under larger synthetic perturbations, CLP-CSGM becomes lower than
interpolation in seven cells at $0.05$ and all nine at $0.10$ and $0.20$;
against smoothing, the corresponding counts are five, eight, and nine. This
is relative robustness of the complete validation-selected procedure, not an
instrument-calibrated noise threshold or an isolated effect of the latent
penalty.

These findings establish a controlled reconstruction result rather than
operational transfer. Cross-well $V_c$ is Larionov-derived from gamma ray,
F03-4 porosity is density-derived, and the sparse observations are subsamples
of those processed curves. The cross-well comparison uses one held-out well
and does not establish operational cross-well or multi-field transferability.
In the separate laboratory-porosity supplement,
CLP-CSGM improves on its decoded conditional prior in every block--seed pair,
but direct AE retains the lower aggregate mean RMSE. The main empirical
lesson is therefore conditional: explicit sparse-measurement refinement can
improve a dense-log-conditioned latent prediction, especially in the
evaluated limited-support configurations, while accurate low-noise
observations may favor substantially simpler reconstruction. Testing that
lesson operationally will require larger multi-well datasets, independently
calibrated targets, geological labels, uncertainty estimates, and faster
refinement strategies.
