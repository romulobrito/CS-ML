\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced CLP-CSGM. A decoder trained on windows of the target curve
supplies the windows the reconstruction may use. The conditional map predicts
a latent code $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ from the dense auxiliary logs. This code
provides an informed initialization and serves as the reference for the
quadratic penalty during latent refinement. Latent refinement balances
agreement with the sparse observations against deviation from
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$. A residual at the observed depths can remain. The
MAP/Tikhonov reading and the local error bound
separate measurement noise, decoder error, a code $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ far from
a good code, and whether
nearby windows can be told apart from the measurements.

Under the paired F3 protocols, CLP-CSGM Ridge has lower mean RMSE than AE
$[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ in seven of nine cross-well cells. On the
published grid that autoencoder has the lowest unweighted mean RMSE among the
direct baselines MLP, PCA, and AE $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$. The
seven-of-nine result is a comparison with this autoencoder, not a ranking
against interpolation or the direct linear maps. This is a conditional
advantage where training support is smallest (steps $16$ and $32$), not overall
superiority: AE
$[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ has lower mean RMSE at step $8$ for
$\rho=0.05$ and $\rho=0.10$. The unweighted mean of the nine cell-level mean
RMSE values decreases from $0.03496$ to $0.02862$, a reduction of approximately
$18.1\%$. In the F03-4 GR-only benchmark, CLP-CSGM Ridge has the lowest mean
RMSE at $\rho\in\{0.20,0.30\}$, while AE $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ has
lower mean RMSE at $\rho\in\{0.40,0.60\}$. That benchmark assimilates the
processed porosity curve with gamma ray as the only dense input; the depth
profile at $\rho=0.20$ follows the broad trend of this curve. It is a
controlled stress test under intentionally incomplete context and is not
evidence of general porosity reconstruction performance. The full Ridge model
has lower mean RMSE than
prior-only in every cross-well cell and than measurement-only CSGM in seven of
nine cells. It also has lower mean RMSE than both ablations throughout the
F03-4 grid. The opposite Ridge--MLP rankings show that the better map from
dense logs to $\boldsymbol{z}_0$ depends on the test.

At the reference noise standard deviation $0.01$, depth interpolation of the
sparse measurements has lower full-window RMSE than CLP-CSGM Ridge in all
nine configurations and in all $27$ seed comparisons. In that regime the
sparse measurements already support a competitive simple solution, which
limits the need for a learned reconstruction. The same windows were then
rescored at noise standard deviations $0.05$, $0.10$, and $0.20$, with the
decoder and the dense-log map held fixed and the penalty reselected at each
level. At $0.05$, about $1.43$ times the held-out curve standard deviation
$0.035$, CLP-CSGM Ridge has the lower mean RMSE at steps $16$ and $32$ and at
step $8$ for $\rho=0.20$. At step $8$ for $\rho=0.05$ and $\rho=0.10$,
interpolation keeps the lower mean. At $0.10$ and $0.20$, about $2.86$ and
$5.71$ times that standard deviation, CLP-CSGM Ridge has the lower error in
all nine configurations. These noise levels were used for sensitivity
analysis and were not calibrated to field measurement uncertainties. AE
$[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$, ordinary least squares, and direct ridge
regression were not rerun across this sweep, and no smoothing comparator was
added, so the change is not a general advantage under noise.

The study presents a latent reconstruction that combines dense-log context
with sparse target observations. The benefit depends on the regime.
The F3 evidence
is reconstruction of processed target logs: cross-well $V_c$ is
Larionov-derived from gamma ray, F03-4 porosity is density-derived, and
sparse observations are coordinate subsamples of these same curves rather than
independently acquired core or laboratory measurements. A separate
single-well supplement on laboratory porosity scores held-out laboratory
coordinates and is not part of that processed-log grid. Relative to the
decoded prediction $G(\boldsymbol{z}_0)$, CLP-CSGM Ridge has lower RMSE in
every split. It also has the lower block-mean RMSE on the two shallower
blocks. On the pooled mean, the direct AE is lower. The larger share of that
error sits in the deepest block. The log differences observed there are
compatible with a transfer difficulty. They do not establish that shift as
the only cause. That supplement is one cored interval. The cross-well
comparison is a controlled single-holdout benchmark on F03-4 and is not
evidence of operational cross-well or multi-field transferability. Broader
assessment will require larger multi-well datasets, independently calibrated
targets, and geological labels. It will also require uncertainty estimates and
faster latent-refinement strategies.
