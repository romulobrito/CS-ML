\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Petrophysical target-log reconstruction builds a continuous property profile
in a target well. Dense wireline logs run along the well, but they measure
the target property only indirectly
\citep{ellis2007well,asquith2004basic,wang2024loggingreview,mondal2022corelog,sun2026corecalibrated}.
A few depths already carry a value of that property. The task is to
reconstruct the remaining depths while penalizing disagreement with those
known values.

Supervised regressors learn a nonlinear map from logs to the target property.
When the sparse observations are available, they are commonly joined to the
dense logs and used as extra inputs
\citep{bishop2006pattern,ahmadi2019comparison,wood2020predicting,pan2023multiwell,mondal2022corelog,koray2024integrated,matinkia2023permpred,abdelazim2022permlogs,shi2024gnsperm,he2025porosity,markus2026hybrid}.
After training, that map is not required to reproduce the observations it was
given. In this work those observations stay measurements: the reconstructed
window is penalized for disagreeing with them, and that penalty does not
force every observation to be met exactly.

Recent studies complete a missing log interval, translate a geomodel property
map, or regress porosity and shale volume from the log suite
\citep{koeshidayatullah2026timegpt,alfakih2026pix2geomodel,alfakih2025wellloggan,alfakih2023northsea}.
Section~\ref{sec:related_work} compares these approaches with the explicit
measurement-fidelity formulation considered here.

An explicit measurement penalty still requires a useful representation of
the complete target window. A fixed sparsity basis can be restrictive when
petrophysical windows vary across wells
\citep{donoho2006compressed,candes2006robust,candes2006stable,bora2017compressed}.
Conditional Latent-Prior Compressed Sensing with Generative Models
(CLP-CSGM) uses a learned decoder to define the candidate windows and a
conditional map to predict the latent anchor
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$ from the dense logs. Test-time optimization then updates
the latent code using the sparse target measurements while penalizing
departure from that anchor. Thus, contextual prediction and measurement
assimilation remain distinct: the dense logs guide the search, whereas the
sparse values directly assess candidate reconstructions. Ridge regression is
the linear low-capacity reference for the conditional map, and an MLP variant
is evaluated under the same channels and split.

The contributions are threefold. First, CLP-CSGM formulates target-log
reconstruction as a conditional latent inverse problem in which the
measurement residual remains active at inference. Second, we use a classical
maximum-a-posteriori (MAP)/Tikhonov interpretation to clarify the
reconstruction objective and a conditional local error bound to identify the
roles of decoder mismatch, latent-prior mismatch, measurement noise, and local
measurement separability. Third, a controlled cross-well benchmark with three
development wells and F03-4 as the single held-out well compares the
formulation with direct predictors and structural ablations
while varying training support, measurement availability, and latent-prior
architecture.

CLP-CSGM Ridge has lower mean root mean square error (RMSE) than the direct
autoencoder in seven of the nine cross-well cells, with the most consistent
gains at steps $16$ and $32$, where the training pools are smaller. The full
model also improves on the conditional prior-only reconstruction in all nine
cells. At the reference noise level, depth interpolation remains lower in all
nine configurations, showing that latent optimization is not always needed;
larger synthetic perturbations yield a more favorable relative ordering for
CLP-CSGM. These findings characterize the tested regimes rather than general
cross-well or operational transferability. Likewise, the local recovery
bound is conditional and does not certify the set-restricted eigenvalue
condition (S-REC) for the trained decoder.
