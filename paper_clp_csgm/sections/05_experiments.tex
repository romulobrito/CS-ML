\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation uses paired protocols for the cross-well low-data $V_c$
benchmark and the single-well F03-4 GR-only porosity benchmark. Within each
cell, all methods share the same partitions, measurement ratios, random seeds,
sampled coordinates, and noise realizations.

\subsection{Cross-well clay-volume
(\texorpdfstring{$V_c$}{Vc}) low-data benchmark}

The cross-well benchmark reconstructs length-$N=64$ processed $V_c$ windows in
the held-out F03-4 well. F02-1, F03-2, and F06-1 provide the model-development
data, and the dense input $\boldsymbol{u}$ concatenates sonic, bulk density,
acoustic impedance, and $V_p$. Reported cross-well performance refers to this
single held-out well. As justified in
Section~\ref{sec:data_eda}, gamma ray is left out because clay volume was
computed from that log
\citep{zhang2025rockphysicslogs}. This is not the log suite for a well in
which gamma ray is available and clay volume is obtained from it directly.

Sparse observations follow
$\boldsymbol{b}=M_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{\eta}$ at
$\rho\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$. Sliding-window steps
$\{8,16,32\}$ control training support, and each step--$\rho$ cell uses three
paired seed realizations. This is a controlled sparse-assimilation benchmark on
a processed target curve, not recovery against an independently acquired
measurement. Split and sampling details are given in
Section~\ref{subsec:train_val_test_protocol}; GR inclusion and the
embargo-style boundary check are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_gr_embargo}.

\subsection{Single-well F03-4 GR-only porosity benchmark}

The second benchmark reconstructs length-$N=64$ windows of the processed
density-derived F03-4 porosity curve using gamma ray as the only dense input.
Acoustic travel time is excluded because it is nearly collinear with the target
(Pearson $r\approx0.9996$) and would provide a near-direct proxy. Density
porosity is linear in bulk density, and a standard sonic porosity is linear
in slowness. If those logs have been recorded, the processed porosity is
already determined, and this test is not a substitute for those calculations.
Gamma ray is then the only dense log, so the dense context is intentionally
incomplete. The target follows the processing of
\citet{zhang2025rockphysicslogs}, in which the density curve underlying F03-4
porosity is itself predicted from sonic and gamma ray.

Sparse observations are sampled at
$\rho\in\{0.20,0.30,0.40,0.50,0.60\}$. Depth is partitioned before window
construction, preventing overlap among training, validation, and test windows.
The benchmark is a controlled test with gamma ray as the only dense log. It is
not a recommendation to interpret porosity from gamma ray alone, and its errors
do not transfer to a suite that also includes density, neutron, and sonic.

The smaller Lapa pre-salt permeability case
\citep{alvarez2025nonlinear} is retained as supplementary evidence in
Appendix~\ref{app:lapa_case}. Appendix~\ref{app:phi_lab_861} reports a
separate single-well check on laboratory porosity. The direct AE has the
lower mean held-out RMSE. Laboratory measurements correct the decoded
prediction of the conditional prior
in every split, and that correction is enough where the test logs remain close
to the support used to fit $h(\boldsymbol{u})$. The check is not part of the main grid.

\subsection{Training, validation, and test protocol}
\label{subsec:train_val_test_protocol}

All preprocessing statistics and model choices are determined without
test-target leakage. Training statistics are reused unchanged on validation and
test windows. The training split fits the direct baselines, autoencoder $(E,G)$,
and latent-prior predictor $h$. Validation targets select $\lambda$ and
baseline-specific hyperparameters, and test targets are used only for final
evaluation.

In the cross-well benchmark, F02-1, F03-2, and F06-1 form the development pool,
F03-4 is held out entirely for testing, and $10\%$ of the development windows
are reserved for validation. Steps $\{8,16,32\}$ produce approximately
$\{104,52,27\}$ windows before this split. At step $32$ the development pool is
small; Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_step32_dev_splits} redraws that pool ten
times with the measurement pattern held fixed. CLP-CSGM Ridge has lower RMSE
than AE $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ at every $\rho$ and in all ten splits.
Because the validation tail is
selected after window construction, boundary windows may overlap when
$\mathrm{step}<N$; Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_gr_embargo} reports an
embargo-style check that removes these overlap-prone windows.

For F03-4 porosity, depth samples are divided into contiguous training,
validation, and test blocks using fractions $60\%$, $20\%$, and $20\%$ before
windows are constructed independently. This depth-first partition prevents
overlap and preserves contiguous-depth generalization.

For each seed and measurement ratio $\rho$, the number of observed coordinates
is
\[
m=\max\{4,\operatorname{round}(\rho L)\}.
\]
We use nearest-integer rounding, with half-integer ties resolved to the nearest
even integer. One coordinate set
$\Omega=\{\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_m\}$ is sampled for each
$(\mathrm{seed},\rho)$ cell and held fixed across all training, validation, and
test windows in that cell. The set defines

\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{b}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
y_{\omega_1} & \cdots & y_{\omega_m}
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
+
\boldsymbol{\eta}.
\label{eq:experimental_sparse_measurements}
\end{equation}

Within each $(\mathrm{seed},\rho)$ cell, all methods share the partitions, the
fixed sampled coordinates, their ordering, and the noise realization. Direct
baselines receive
$[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]\in\mathbb{R}^{p+m}$, with only the measured
target values in the ordering of $M_{\Omega}$. Because $\Omega$ is fixed within
the cell, the $j$-th entry of $\boldsymbol{b}$ consistently corresponds to
coordinate $\omega_j$ across windows, making coordinate identity implicit in
the stable input ordering. No zero-padded target, binary mask, or measurement
matrix is added. CLP-CSGM uses $M_{\Omega}$ explicitly in
$\|M_{\Omega}G(\boldsymbol{z})-\boldsymbol{b}\|_2^2$. The protocol therefore
compares direct fusion with operator-based assimilation under a fixed sampling
pattern, giving the direct models a stable coordinate-to-input mapping within
each cell. Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_explicit_mask} gives one direct AE the
same measurements on the window coordinates plus a binary mask. That mask does
not remove the CLP-CSGM advantage at steps $16$ and $32$; at step $8$ the
masked AE remains better at $\rho=0.05$ and $\rho=0.10$, and the mask does not
uniformly lower the direct AE error. This comparator is not part of the main
grid.

Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_omega_placement} redraws the coordinate set five
times with the model seeds held fixed. Where training support is smallest
(steps $16$ and $32$), CLP-CSGM Ridge keeps the lower RMSE in every draw.
At step $8$, where the development pool is largest, the ranking is less
stable: AE $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ stays ahead at $\rho=0.05$ and
on the majority of draws at $\rho=0.10$, while CLP-CSGM stays ahead on the
majority of draws at $\rho=0.20$. The cell-level winner still matches the
published ranking in all nine cells. That check is not part of the main grid.

Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_simple_baselines} adds direct linear and ridge
maps from $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$ to the window, and depth
interpolation of the sparse measurements, on this same cross-well grid.
It also raises the measurement-noise standard deviation above the published
value $0.01$. Section~\ref{sec:results} summarizes that noise dependence
for interpolation and CLP-CSGM Ridge. The linear maps and the per-seed
tables are not merged into the submitted nine-cell grid.

Noise is applied after subsampling, with
$\sigma_{\eta}=0.01$ for cross-well $V_c$ and
$\sigma_{\eta}=0.02$ for F03-4 porosity, independently across coordinates and
windows.

For CLP-CSGM, the first latent-refinement run starts from
$\boldsymbol{z}_0=h(\boldsymbol{u})$. Each additional run starts at
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$ plus an independent draw from
$\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},0.05^{2}I)$. The penalty center remains
$\boldsymbol{z}_0$ on every run and every iteration, and the candidate with
the lowest objective is retained. The measurement-only ablation sets
$\lambda=0$ and starts from zero, whereas the prior-only variant returns the
decoded prediction of the conditional prior, $G(h(\boldsymbol{u}))$, with no
latent search. Predictions are returned to the original scale using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:inverse_target_standardization}.

\subsection{Baselines and evaluation metrics}
\label{subsec:baselines_metrics}

The direct baselines are ML only, which uses only $\boldsymbol{u}$; MLP
$[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$, which predicts the target directly; PCA
$[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$, which predicts target principal-component
scores; and AE $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$, which predicts codes of a
separately trained target autoencoder. CLP-CSGM Ridge is the proposed reference
configuration.
Prior-only Ridge and measurement-only CSGM are structural ablations, while the
MLP latent prior is used only for sensitivity analysis.

Metrics are computed after inverse standardization on the original target
scale. RMSE is the primary metric and selects $\lambda$ on validation data;
MAE and relative $\ell_2$ error provide complementary summaries. Results are
computed under the paired protocol for each seed and then summarized across
seeds, with lower values indicating better reconstruction.

\paragraph{Uncertainty summaries.}
All seed-level intervals use the three fixed seeds $\{7,23,41\}$. Reported
$95\%$ half-widths are Student-$t$ intervals with
$\mathrm{df}=n_{\mathrm{seeds}}-1=2$ (critical value
$t_{2,0.975}\approx 4.303$), not normal $z=1.96$ intervals. Where CLP-CSGM
Ridge is compared with AE $[\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{b}]$, we also report the
paired seed-wise difference
\[
\Delta\mathrm{RMSE}
=
\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{AE}}
-
\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{CLP}},
\]
together with its seed-level standard error and Student-$t$ half-width.
Positive $\Delta\mathrm{RMSE}$ favors CLP-CSGM Ridge. With only three seeds,
these intervals describe seed-level reproducibility within the paired protocol;
their interpretation remains limited by the small seed panel.
Two supplements widen that evidence and leave the three-seed tables as the
main result.
Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_seed_panel} repeats the comparison on ten seeds.
Each extra seed refits the models and draws a new coordinate set, so training
randomness and observation placement change together. None of those nine
intervals include zero. The CLP-CSGM advantage remains where training support
is smallest (steps $16$ and $32$); AE remains better at step $8$ for
$\rho=0.05$ and $\rho=0.10$.
Appendix~\ref{app:crosswell_omega_placement} holds the same three model seeds
and redraws only the coordinate set. The advantage at steps $16$ and $32$
holds in every draw, and the step-$8$ ranking is the less stable one.
Overlapping windows inside a well are not counted as further independent
samples.
